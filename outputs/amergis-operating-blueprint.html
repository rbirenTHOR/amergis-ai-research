A task-level blueprint for staffing operations

Public evidence supports an AI-operated administrative layer. Human professionals still deliver care and education, and the design reserves professional judgment, attestations and defined exceptions to named owners. This is a research and implementation specification, not a deployed system.

Proposed authority across 63 tasks

Task counts do not estimate labor hours, employee reductions or financial savings. All A tasks require explicit delegation, verified inputs and exception routing.

What was reconstructed

Two public job catalogs, a listed-office network, role responsibilities, public product workflows, commercial rules and selected vendor disclosures.

The private candidate pool, customer CRM, production schema, realized margins and actual staffing levels are still unknown.

The role map covers 19 characterized families and one unresolved Staffing Analyst family.

Findings that change the design

Contract-specific logic is essential. A single customer’s holiday calendar, time approval process or invoice clock must not become a company-wide rule. Missing or contradictory terms stay unresolved until an owner clarifies them.

    Release evidence needed before live operation

    • Approved client/worker data, operating scope, vendor connections and contract-rule versions.
    • Named owners and response deadlines for clinical, employment, payroll and contract exceptions.
    • Scenario results and real error/override measurements meeting thresholds set by those owners.
    • Permission boundaries, approval-version binding, idempotency and an exercised rollback path.
    • Role/jurisdiction-specific requirements resolved for the pilot, including credential and employment processes.
